WHAT TRACKING SUCCESS DOES NOT TELL YOU

Tracking success alone does not identify which information a learned command interface supplies to the controller.

Humanoid systems increasingly compress human motion into a learned command space, and the natural way to validate that choice is downstream task success. But success is compatible with more than one explanation. In particular there is a confound that is easy to leave untested: the time index. If the command is a function of frame number and the clip is familiar, a clock is a sufficient explanation of the score — no motion content required. That is not a hypothetical: it is what happened to us, on our own latent.

THE INSTRUMENT

Five students. One exclusive interface. The differences are the measurement.

An exclusivity contract guarantees the reference reaches the actor only through the 64-d channel — a causal leak detector caught and repaired the one violation. Five students train under an identical frozen recipe; only the channel's content differs. They are evaluated on 69 reference-only paired starts whose present states are close but whose futures diverge, so a command encoding only progress-through-clip cannot pick the right future.

THE MEASUREMENT

Every interval excludes zero. Both clips. Both nulls. Every seed.  measured · closed

Paired per-cluster effects with hierarchical 95% CIs; the preregistered temporal-block bootstrap (12 blocks, 10,000 replicates) closes the autocorrelation door. All displayed numbers are machine-generated from the frozen analyzer whose SHA-256 is stamped into the paper's macros.

Per-seed effects vs time: +0.154 / +0.278 / +0.140 — positive at every training seed, on each clip.

Destroy the channel. Competence vanishes.

Zero it, shuffle it, or replace it with marginal-random samples: general completion collapses from 0.923 to exactly 0.000 — at all three seeds, in all three destruction modes, on both the explicit and latent arms.

Two claims live here and they have different standing. Every bit of goal information flows through this channel — that is architecture, provable from the decoder's input width, and it needs no experiment. What the experiment adds is that the decoder has no proprioception-only fallback: raw proprioception still reaches it, and it still cannot act. What the experiment does not show is that the channel's content is motion semantics — it cannot separate goal content from state-coupled content. A related measurement suggests the latter is substantial: merely holding the command for 0.1 s drops the explicit student from 0.926 to 0.223, where a channel carrying only slow motion semantics should barely notice.

THE PROPOSED METHOD

MorphoRetarget: read the robot's specification, not its name  proposed · no result

Retargeting today is an optimization with a hand-written config per robot. The ambition here is one network that reads an unseen humanoid's identity-free physical specification and emits motion that survives independent physics verification — no robot ID, no per-robot output head, no learned prompt, no re-tuned IK weights. This section describes a design and a working implementation. It reports no experimental outcome. Every number below is an architectural or contract fact, never a performance fact.

“A single retargeter reads an unseen humanoid's physical specification, rather than its identity, and produces motion that survives independent physics verification.”

Formally ŶR = Fθ(XH, R, O), where R is a versioned RobotSpec bundling asset, kinematics, geometry, dynamics, actuation, semantics and runtime — and the identity fields are stripped before the network ever sees it.

Architecture

snmr/robot_spec.py · snmr/robot_tokens.py · snmr/morpho_root_prior.py · autoresearch/iterate-260901-1222/a1_root_prior_trajectory_prereg.json

Two things this architecture does not do — stated so nobody misreads the diagram

It is not dynamics-conditioned. Mass, inertia, torque and velocity limits, armature, damping, kp and kd all exist in the 37-feature RobotSpec bundle, but the registered experiment consumes the kinematic feature set only — dynamics_available is zero for these batches. Nothing the network has seen could teach it about torque.

It does not learn the root trajectory outright. The root is an analytic leg-length-ratio prior plus a bounded learned residual. "The network predicts the root" would overstate it.

And there are no simulator gradients anywhere in the critical path. Newton's MuJoCo-Warp solver is not differentiable and the Isaac Lab integration is beta, so the differentiable-physics stage from the original design was deliberately dropped rather than faked.

The verification ladder

Retargeting and tracking are two layers, and conflating them is how a weak tracker gets blamed on a retargeter. So verification is staged, and each rung is reported separately.

L0

Forward-kinematics contract

10,000 deterministic in-limit poses across 7 links, checked cross-backend against mujoco.mj_forward. Max link error 3.93e-6 m, max quaternion geodesic 3.76e-6 rad. This rung is genuinely done.

measured · closed
L1

Kinematic feasibility screen

Joint/velocity/acceleration limits, self-collision, ground penetration, foot skate, contact consistency, COM support margin, inverse-dynamics torque margin. Roughly 1 CPU-second per clip, needs no controller — the physics evidence a single-GPU lab can actually afford. Designed and specified; never run on any model output.

proposed · no result
L2

Frozen-tracker rollout

A per-robot tracking policy, frozen, used as a selective oracle over candidate references. This verifier does not exist yet. There is no eval code path, and both trackers that do exist are single-clip specialists — a specialist cannot rank arbitrary retargeted motion.

proposed · no result
L3

Downstream utility — retrain the tracker

Identical recipe, identical seeds, identical budget; vary only the motion source (GMR vs learned vs learned+repair). This is the only experiment in the program that could support the phrase “improves tracking performance,” and it has not been started. ~138 GPU-hours at the current frozen recipe.

Five preregistered screens, one anchor, and a gate the teacher itself fails  negative · kept

The stop, exactly

The final model passed the FK bound (0.0724 m against a 0.073 m ceiling — by 0.56 mm), recorded zero joint-limit violations, and landed within 3.4% of GMR on the human-side semantic ruler. It was stopped by one anchor on one clip: ground1_subject5 / right_foot at amplitude ratio 1.51 against a frozen upper bound of 1.50.

The report's own machine-written reason label is model_amplitude_collapse. That label is wrong, and the error has already propagated into the governing status document: 1.51 exceeds an upper bound, so this is high-side amplification, not collapse. It inverts the finding.

And a second, independent blocker — fixing the gate is not enough

Replaying the registered qualification statistic on the screen data gives a point estimate of 1.034 with a paired clip-bootstrap CI of [1.015, 1.057] against a rule demanding an upper bound ≤ 1.05 — a miss of 0.007 driven by clip heterogeneity (per-clip ratios span 0.98–1.11), not by seed noise. Thirteen validation clips cannot resolve a 5% non-inferiority margin; roughly 22+ are needed at this point estimate, and averaging more seeds shrinks the wrong variance term. (Indicative: seed-0, 20k-step data standing in for a 3-seed, 50k-step registered statistic — a power diagnosis, not a computed qualification failure.)

THE LARGEST EFFECT IN THE PROGRAM

Robustness is a training recipe, not a representation  measured · single seed

A frozen student whose reference stream drops out mid-rollout falls apart. The obvious hypothesis — that a better command representation degrades more gracefully — is one we tested and killed: under dropout, the content-free clock is the most robust arm at every severe cell, and a stale explicit reference is worse than no reference at all, falling below the goal-blind floor. Cross-arm dropout rankings measure channel reliance plus an active-harm term, not command quality.

The effect that is real belongs to training. One recipe change — reference-stream dropout during training, with a naive hold fill — buys back almost everything, at no clean cost:

Why this is the punchline, not a footnote

Every masked arm — explicit, flag-free explicit, live clock, frozen clock, goal-window, shuffled — lands within about 0.1 of every other. The masked-versus-frozen effect is +0.45. The training recipe dominates the choice of command representation by roughly four to one. A whole program of representation contrasts sits inside the noise of one recipe change.

Related, and also negative: the validity flag bits are not the mechanism (the flag-free arm is equal or better), and the latent-specific version of this effect was killed by its own preregistered stop rule at +0.016 against a +0.05 threshold — losing to a live clock by 0.066.

Three caveats that travel with these numbers

Seed 0 only. There is no confidence interval on +0.447 and it has not been replicated.

Do not quote floor-relative retention from this experiment. The original "never falls below its floor" claim was retracted the same day it was written — against the correct goal-blind floor the retention at the severest cell is negative. Only the paired Δ column above is safe.

This is not an SNMR result. The winning arms are the explicit and clock arms. The latent branch is the one that was killed.

The holdout that failed, kept in view

Leave-one-robot-out on EngineAI PM01, on the legacy 8-D-conditioned pipeline: 29.59 cm zero-shot against 5.72 cm in-training — a 5.2× gap against a ≤2× criterion. This is the failure that motivates the whole RobotSpec design. It belongs to the old architecture, and the exact checkpoint is missing, so the mechanism was never localized. It has not yet been re-run under MorphoRetarget, because MorphoRetarget has never been evaluated on a held-out robot at all.

The downstream comparison that exists — and what it does and does not say

Training the same tracker on GMR data versus SNMR data gave completion 0.867 either way, a delta of +0.0 pp with a 95% CI of [−5.8, +6.2] against a −5.0 pp non-inferiority margin. Verdict: noninferiority_not_established, failing by 0.83 pp of CI width.

This is a power result, not a quality gap — and more rollouts will not shrink it, because the dominant variance term is between-policy. It must not be cited as establishing equivalence, and it is nowhere near enough to support the phrase "improves tracking performance."
